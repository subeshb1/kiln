From the pass

One fire. One menu. No shortcuts hiding anywhere.

We built this room around a six-foot hearth and let it make the rules. No gas, no induction — if it can't be cooked over oak, it isn't on the menu. That constraint is the whole point: fire makes you patient, and patience is most of cooking.

Nearly everything comes from within two hours of the door — the beef is dry-aged downstairs, the flour is milled in the Peaks, and the damsons were someone's garden surplus. The menu changes when the ingredients do, not when the calendar says so.

— Rhea Calder, chef & owner

The tasting menu.

£85 · wine pairing £60 · seven courses, one pace

  1. Oyster, smoked cream, apple

    Cold from the ice, warm from the smoke. One bite, start here.

  2. Sourdough, ember butter

    Baked at four, split over the coals at seven. The butter is whipped with roast bones.

  3. Leeks, cured yolk, hazelnut

    Buried in the ash until soft all the way through, then dressed like they earned it.

  4. Coal-roasted celeriac, whey, black garlic

    The vegetable course people write to us about.

  5. Turbot, sauce of the grilled bones

    On the bone, over the fire, basted until it gives. The sauce is everything left over.

  6. Aged beef, burnt onion, marrow butter

    Sixty days downstairs, six minutes over oak. Rest longer than feels reasonable.

  7. Ember-set cream, preserved damson, bay ice

    Custard set beside the dying fire while the room finishes its wine.

The menu changes with the moon-ish — roughly monthly. Tell us about allergies when you book; the fire can work around almost anything except regret.
